MATHS — a systematic
trading process, run as software

MATHS is the second product of Data Concept Studio: a rules-based process for liquid US equities and major crypto assets. Four independent models — technical, fundamentals, sentiment and macro — each form a view; a fixed rule combines them; and every candidate position passes an independent risk gate that no model can override.

It runs in production on the firm's own capital while we build the evidence that would have to precede any wider release. MATHS is not offered to anyone today, publishes no performance figures, and is not investment advice — what this is, and what it is not.

Investment philosophy

A process is more honest than a memory

We believe markets are mostly efficient and occasionally, briefly, not — and that those exceptions are easier to find with a disciplined, repeatable process than with conviction. A rule that is written down can be measured, criticised and improved. A hunch cannot. That is the whole case for a systematic approach: not that models are smarter than people, but that a process can be held to account in a way a memory cannot.

We have chosen breadth over concentration. Many modest, independently reasoned positions can be evaluated statistically; a handful of large bets cannot, and their outcomes say almost nothing about whether the reasoning behind them was sound. The cost of that choice is real, and we accept it: we give up the big story and keep the evidence.

The hard part is not finding a signal. It is doing the same thing every day without drift — not overriding the rule on a bad morning, not quietly relaxing a limit after a good month. Most of the engineering in MATHS exists to make discipline the default and to make any departure from it visible.

The investment process

Once through, in order, every run

Each stage takes a defined input and produces a defined output. Nothing skips a stage, and the gate at the end is not a formality.

01 · UNIVERSE AND DATA

Defined by liquidity, not by a view

The investable universe is set by liquidity and listing criteria. Prices, filings, positioning data, analyst revisions, volatility and macro indicators are collected on a fixed schedule, and every input is timestamped so a later review can reconstruct exactly what the process knew, and when.

02 · SIGNALS AND FORECASTS

Four views, one fixed rule

Four independent models — technical, fundamentals, sentiment and macro — each score every name without seeing the others' output. A fixed, reviewed rule combines them into a single view per name. The weights are set deliberately and change only through the research process, never on the fly.

03 · PORTFOLIO CONSTRUCTION

Sized together, not one at a time

Positions are sized relative to one another. Correlated names share a risk budget, and limits on single names, aggregate exposure and concentration are inputs to construction rather than checks applied afterwards. An independent risk gate then has the final word on every candidate.

04 · IMPLEMENTATION AND OVERSIGHT

Defined entry, stop and target

Every order carries a defined entry, stop and target, and the firm's records are reconciled against the broker's continuously. A person can halt the process at any time; the gate itself has no override. Every decision, including each model's individual view, is logged permanently.

Risk management

Limits are inputs, not afterthoughts

Risk control in MATHS is part of how a portfolio is built, not a report produced once it exists. These are the controls that actually run.

Diversification first

Breadth across names and across independent model views does more to limit damage than any single stop. It is the reason the rest of the framework can afford to be strict.

Limits shape the portfolio

Per-name, aggregate-exposure and concentration limits are inputs to construction. A position that would breach a limit is not trimmed after the fact — it is never built.

A gate with no override

An independent risk gate sits between the portfolio and the market. It applies hard rules on volatility, upcoming events and liquidity that no model — and no operator in a hurry — can override. One failure stops everything.

Standing aside is a decision

When conditions fall outside the process's defined operating range, it stops opening positions. Holding cash is an intended outcome, not an outage, and it is logged as such.

Reconciled against the broker

The firm's record of positions and orders is checked against the broker's continuously. A discrepancy is treated as a halt condition until it is explained — never as noise.

Every decision attributable

Each model's view, the combined forecast, the sizing and the gate's verdict are recorded for every name on every run. Any position can be traced back to the inputs that produced it.

Research process

Most candidates do not make it

Every change to the process follows the same loop: a stated hypothesis, measurement on data the process has not seen, and a promotion rule a candidate must clear before it replaces what is in production. Most candidates do not clear it, and that is the point of having the rule.

A result we dropped

In 2026 we tested letting the model weights adapt automatically from closed trades. Out of sample, the adaptive weights did not clear our bar against a fixed, reviewed set. So the weights in production stay fixed, and the adaptive version remains a research line rather than a feature. We would rather publish that than imply the system learns on its own.

Currently under research, without dates: exit rules that respond to how a position develops after entry, and the cost of trading measured against the edge that has to justify it.

Technology

What the engineering buys

The process runs without anyone present. The environment it runs in is defined as code and reviewed before it changes; there is no manual path to production. MATHS runs on Google Cloud, which is what lets a very small firm operate a continuous process with a reliability that would otherwise need a team.

The same cycle — collect, forecast, construct, implement, reconcile — runs the same way on a larger balance at essentially the same cost. That is what the engineering is for: not speed, but repeatability.
