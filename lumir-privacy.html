1. The short version

Lumir is a mirror and screen-light app. It runs entirely on your iPhone.

As of the source checked on 2026-09-12, the Lumir app contains no networking code at all — no URLSession, no URLRequest, no dataTask, no NWConnection, no CFNetwork, and no third-party SDK or package dependency of any kind. Every framework it imports is a first-party Apple framework. There is therefore no code path by which Lumir sends your camera preview, your photos, your presets or your settings anywhere.

There are three honest qualifications to that, and they are set out in full in section 6 rather than buried:

  • Buying or restoring Lumir Pro involves Apple. StoreKit — Apple's own framework, not Lumir's code — communicates with the App Store to take payment and to confirm what you own.
  • Photos you capture go into your photo library, and from there your own iCloud Photos settings apply, not Lumir's.
  • Your Lumir files are included in your device backup, because they live in Application Support. If you back up to iCloud, they go where your backup goes.

Lumir has no account, no sign-in, no password and no analytics. It asks for nothing about you.

3. What Lumir collects

Nothing.

Apple's own definition is the useful one here. Apple's App Privacy Details guidance defines "collect" as "transmitting data off the device in a way that allows you and/or your third-party partners to access it for a period longer than what is necessary to service the transmitted request in real time," and states that "data that is processed only on device is not 'collected' and does not need to be disclosed." (Accessed 2026-09-12 — developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details.)

By that definition Lumir collects no data, because Lumir transmits no data. This is not a policy commitment that someone has to keep; as of the code checked, it is a property of the binary, because the app contains no mechanism for transmitting anything.

[CONTROLLER ENTITY — OC-06] therefore receives no personal data from the app: no name, no email address, no device identifier, no advertising identifier, no location, no contact list, no usage history, no crash report, no photo and no camera frame.

4. What Lumir stores, and where

Lumir writes three JSON files into its own sandboxed ~/Library/Application Support/Lumir/ directory on your iPhone. Nothing else is written anywhere. They are readable only by Lumir and by you (through a device backup); no other app can reach them.

FileWhat is in itWhere it comes from
presets.json Your custom presets only: a generated UUID, the name you typed, a colour temperature in kelvin, a brightness value, created/updated timestamps and a revision counter. You, when you create a preset. Built-in presets are compiled into the app and are never written to disk.
settings.json Your chosen layout (full / split-top / split-bottom), the id of the last preset you selected, and whether you have allowed Lumir to adjust screen brightness. Your own choices in the app.
entitlement.json Whether you own Lumir Pro: the product identifier, the StoreKit original transaction identifier, the purchase date, and when StoreKit last verified it. Apple's StoreKit, after you purchase.

The only free text Lumir ever stores is a preset name you typed yourself. There is no other field anywhere in which arbitrary text about you could be recorded.

entitlement.json deserves a specific statement, because purchase records are where privacy policies usually get vague: it contains no payment details, no card number, no billing address, no Apple Account identifier and no name. It holds only the transaction identity that StoreKit itself already placed on the device, and it exists for one narrow reason — so that a temporary StoreKit failure does not silently take Pro away from someone who paid for it.

Backups. This directory is part of the normal device backup, deliberately, so that restoring your iPhone does not silently lose the presets you made. That means: if you use iCloud Backup, these three files are in your iCloud backup, under your Apple Account and Apple's terms, not Lumir's. This is the one respect in which the phrase "nothing leaves your device" needs qualifying, and it is your backup doing it, at your choice.

5. Camera, photos and screen light

5.1 The camera

Lumir asks for camera access so it can show you a mirrored preview of the front camera. iOS shows you the app's stated reason, which is currently: "Lumir shows a mirrored preview from the front camera. Nothing leaves your device."

Traced in the code, because that is a strong claim. Lumir's capture session has exactly one output attached to it: an AVCapturePhotoOutput. There is no AVCaptureVideoDataOutput, no AVCaptureMovieFileOutput, no AVCaptureAudioDataOutput and no sample-buffer delegate anywhere in the app — so there is no code path through which an individual camera frame can even be read by Lumir, let alone stored or sent. The live preview is drawn by AVCaptureVideoPreviewLayer, which renders directly from the capture session to the screen.

No microphone. Lumir adds only the front video device as an input. It never requests microphone access and records no audio.

Center Stage is left alone. Lumir neither reads nor writes any Center Stage setting; whatever your own Control Center says stays in force.

5.2 Photos

When you tap the shutter, Lumir captures one photo and saves it to your photo library.

  • The capture happens only when you ask for it. Nothing is captured in the background, on a timer, or when the app is not in the foreground.
  • Lumir requests add-only photo library permission (NSPhotoLibraryAddUsageDescription). It cannot read, browse, search or delete your existing photos, and it does not ask for permission that would let it.
  • The photo is saved mirrored, matching what you saw on screen.
  • The saved photo is then an ordinary item in your photo library. What happens to it next — iCloud Photos, Shared Albums, anything else — is governed by your own iOS settings, not by Lumir.
  • Lumir keeps only the photo library's local asset identifier in memory for the current session, so it can confirm the save succeeded. It is not written to disk.

5.3 Screen light

The light panel is just the screen. Lumir adjusts screen brightness while you use it, if you allow it. Nothing about that is recorded beyond the on/off preference stored in settings.json.

6. What does reach Apple, and why

Lumir sends nothing. Apple, however, is unavoidably involved in two places, and pretending otherwise would make this document false.

6.1 Your purchase. Lumir Pro is bought through Apple's In-App Purchase, as Apple's App Review Guideline 3.1.1 requires for unlocking features (accessed 2026-09-12 — developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines). Apple takes the payment; Lumir never sees and never receives your payment details. Apple Media Services Terms and Conditions (Poland edition, last updated 15 September 2025, accessed 2026-09-12 — apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/pl/terms.html) name Apple Distribution International Ltd. as the registered seller of App Store content for customers in Poland. Apple's handling of your purchase is covered by Apple's own privacy policy and terms, not by this one.

Concretely: buying, restoring or re-verifying Pro means StoreKit — Apple's framework, running on your device — talking to Apple's servers (Product.products(for:), product.purchase(), AppStore.sync(), Transaction.currentEntitlements, Transaction.updates). Those are the network exchanges that exist in Lumir's user experience. They are Apple's, they are between you and Apple, and they carry your purchase — not your presets, your settings, your photos or your camera.

6.2 Apple's reporting to the developer. Separately from the app, Apple provides developers with App Store sales figures and, for those users who have opted in to Share With App Developers, aggregated App Analytics. Apple states that usage data "only include data from users who have agreed to share their diagnostics and usage information with app developers" and applies minimum thresholds and differential privacy to protect individuals (accessed 2026-09-12 — developer.apple.com/app-store/app-analytics). This is aggregate reporting produced by Apple, controlled by your iOS settings, and it identifies no individual to [CONTROLLER ENTITY — OC-06]. Lumir itself contributes nothing to it.

7. Third parties, analytics, advertising and tracking

There are none. Specifically, and each verified by search rather than assumed:

  • No third-party SDKs. Lumir has no Swift Package dependencies, no CocoaPods, no Carthage. Every import in the app is an Apple framework: AVFoundation, CoreGraphics, Foundation, Observation, Photos, QuartzCore, StoreKit, SwiftUI, UIKit.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting service. No PostHog, Mixpanel, Firebase, Amplitude, Segment, Sentry, Crashlytics, AppsFlyer, Bugsnag or Datadog — nothing.
  • No advertising, no advertising identifier, no App Tracking Transparency prompt. Lumir never reads the advertising identifier or the vendor identifier, and it has no tracking behaviour to ask permission for.
  • No data is shared with anyone, because none is collected. Apple App Review Guideline 5.1.1(i) requires a privacy policy to confirm that any third party with whom an app shares user data provides equivalent protection. Lumir shares user data with no third party, so there is nothing to confirm.

One structural detail worth stating, because it is unusual and it is load-bearing. Lumir contains an internal analytics interface — deliberately switched off — so that if telemetry is ever added it must be added deliberately, reviewed and disclosed, rather than switched on quietly. What ships is NoOpAnalyticsSink, which does nothing at all. More importantly, the event type AnalyticsEvent is a closed enum in which no case carries a String, Data or image payload. Every case carries either nothing or a fixed enumerated value. A preset name, a photo or a line of free text has no channel it could travel through, even in a future version — not because a rule forbids it, but because the type system does not permit it. For example, the event for using a custom preset records that a custom preset was used and deliberately carries no way to say which.

8. Retention, deletion, and how you get your data out

Apple's App Review Guideline 5.1.1(i) requires a privacy policy to explain data retention and deletion and how a user can revoke consent or request deletion. Here is the whole of it.

Retention. Your presets and settings stay on your device until you delete them. Nothing expires and nothing is uploaded, so there is no server-side copy with its own retention period.

Deletion, and you do not have to ask anyone.

  • Delete one preset — delete it in the app. Deleting is never restricted, whether or not you have Lumir Pro. You can always remove something you made.
  • Delete everything — delete the Lumir app. iOS removes the app's container, which removes presets.json, settings.json and entitlement.json with it.
  • Photos you captured are yours, in your photo library, and are deleted there like any other photo. Deleting Lumir does not delete them.
  • Withdraw camera or photo access at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security, or in Settings → Lumir. Lumir remains usable as a light without camera access; that is a designed mode, not a degraded one.

There is no deletion request to send, because [CONTROLLER ENTITY — OC-06] holds nothing to delete. If that ever stops being true — for example if the optional preset sync described in section 11 is ever built — this policy changes first, before the feature ships.
